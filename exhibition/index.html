01 / THE IDEA

The web is more than a screen.

In the 1990s, artists began using the internet as more than a place to display information. They experimented with hyperlinks, participation, and code as parts of the artwork. This exhibition brings together three works that challenge the idea that a website should be simple to read and easy to use. In Olia Lialina’s My Boyfriend Came Back from the War, clicking through separate frames turns a story into a fragmented experience. Douglas Davis’s The World’s First Collaborative Sentence invites visitors to add to a sentence, making the audience part of the work and questioning who gets to be its author. JODI’s ASDFG disrupts normal browsing through unusual text, code, and visual behavior. Each work uses a different strategy—narrative, participation, or disruption—but all ask viewers to do more than passively look at a webpage. Together, they show how early net art made the website itself a place for experimentation.
